My work focuses on trustworthy AI for health research and on tools that automate the analysis of real-world data (RWD).

I study causal reasoning in AI models, evaluate AI systems for health research, and develop methods to estimate causal effects from real-world health data. I have hands-on experience in target trial emulation. Using US Medicare claims, I emulated the ONTARGET trial comparing telmisartan with ramipril. I hold a PhD in econometrics and am a PhD candidate in health informatics at the University of Missouri. My clinical research interests include hypertension, Alzheimer’s disease, and cancer.

Current research

TrialCalibre for target trial automation

I am developing TrialCalibre, an AI system for automating target trial emulation with real-world health data. It is designed to turn a clinical research question into a study protocol, carry that protocol through analysis and reporting, and let researchers review and verify decisions at each stage. I am extending it to benchmark observational estimates against randomized trials and assess when calibration is appropriate.

Causal machine learning and heterogeneous treatment effects

I completed a Medicare emulation of ONTARGET, a randomized trial comparing telmisartan with ramipril. I use causal machine learning (causal ML), including causal forests, to study heterogeneous treatment effects: how the effect of one treatment compared with another varies across patients. I am investigating whether these patterns recur in both the randomized trial and Medicare data.

Causal reasoning in AI

I use falsification-based stress tests to assess causal reasoning in large language models (LLMs).

AI evaluation in health

I evaluate how reliably AI systems carry out clinical research tasks. For example, in the TrialCode Agent study, we measured how accurately AI-generated clinical code sets matched expert-built reference sets.
